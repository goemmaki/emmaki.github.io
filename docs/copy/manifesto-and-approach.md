> Too many people, when confronted with the ogre of AI, default to old ways of thinking about the value they bring to the table. They hear the clamor of the anti-AI crowd, they fear a future where AI takes it all.
>
> And maybe they're right. But the tumult of every industrial revolution eventually passes, and a new way of working with its new values and secure ways of living settle. Our problem right now, whether you love AI, hate it, or are simply indifferent to it, is that we don't understand the future and our place in it.
>
> That really is terrifying. Well, I'm in year 3 of the AI atomizing the writing industry, and I've spent the last three years learning. My value isn't that I give you 'human' writing enriched with my experience as a person. My value isn't that I can build automated systems, or even that I'm a good prompt-pusher (it's how I built this website!)
>
> My value is that I know when to be creative and when to let the machine chug away. AI slop is a result of so-called creatives letting the machine chug away for them. People who will decry the loss of creativity - yet turn to to the writing machine to turn their perfectly impefect thoughts into a sludge of generic confusion. Because it is easier. Because it looks more professional.
>
> I fight against this, just as I fight against corporations abusing AI for the worst version of our K-shaped future on rails. Writing may have been one of the first industries to fall to AI in 2023 as ChatGPT cracked the door of a brave new world open.
>
> You could call me a Luddite.
>
> But AI cannot test what is subjective. It cannot create original insights. It can only infer, deterioriating into confusion as its memory escapes it. My value is that I am the one who makes it my job to understand how to use AI to create something original.
>
> In the end, I have the same argument as everyone else. My value is my humanity.
