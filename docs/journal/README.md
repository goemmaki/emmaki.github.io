# Development journal

This is the factual record of how the Emmaki site was developed. It is source material for the owner’s case study, not the case study itself.

## Updating it

Append one dated entry to the history file for every working session. Record what changed, what was tried and removed, who directed and implemented it, problems and corrections, and the evidence available. Keep earlier entries intact; correct the record in a later dated note if new evidence changes it.

## Record rules

- Tie every historical claim to a commit hash or a transcript date. Use **inferred** for conclusions drawn by comparing evidence rather than stated directly.
- Treat commits as evidence of code state, not proof of who authored the implementation. Use transcripts and audit reports for attribution, rationale, measurements, and review.
- Be candid about failed attempts, reversals, mistakes, and uncertainty. Do not convert an incomplete audit into a passing result.
- Include measured numbers only with their date and method. Distinguish an estimate or tool-reported cost from an actual charge.
- Do not include marketing copy, conclusions about what AI can do, or case-study prose. The owner writes the case study and decides what is impressive.
- Keep private details out. Refer to the owner as “the owner”; do not include secrets, email addresses, client names/data, or Fiverr buyer conversations.
